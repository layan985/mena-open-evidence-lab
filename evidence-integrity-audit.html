What is checked

The audit applies the same provenance and comparability discipline used in the Lab's public research infrastructure.

Source identity

Producer, release, retrieval date, vintage and whether the cited source actually supports the claim.

Statistical identity

Population, denominator, geography, concept, unit, reference period, base/reference year and adjustment status.

Method breaks

Survey redesigns, rebasing, classification changes, revised historical series and other discontinuities that affect comparison.

Transformations

Rebasing, aggregation, joins, deflation, growth calculations and other transformations checked for traceability and reproducibility.

Claim boundaries

What the evidence can support, what needs qualification, and what should be removed or rewritten.

Reproducibility

Whether another analyst can reconstruct material numbers from the supplied sources and documented steps.

Deliverable

A concise evidence ledger with one row per material claim: green where the chain is defensible, amber where qualification or reconstruction is required, and red where the published claim cannot be supported from the supplied evidence.

Each material issue includes the source, failure mode, corrected statistical object where recoverable, and suggested replacement wording. A change log records any corrections made during the audit.

Good fit

Evaluation reports, donor studies, market assessments, country comparisons, policy dashboards, investment or risk notes, and research outputs using official MENA statistics.

What this is not

The audit does not certify an entire organization, guarantee that a conclusion is true, or replace substantive domain review. It audits the evidence chain for the material claims in scope.
