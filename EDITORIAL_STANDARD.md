# Research Editorial & Chart Standard

_Last updated: 16 August 2026_

Design exists to make evidence easier to inspect. It does not substitute for evidence.

## Publication system

The Lab uses a restrained research-editorial system across reports, briefs, methods notes, country rooms and web data cards. Templates must support dense tables, technical appendices, source notes and evidence labels without converting research outputs into promotional graphics.

## Chart grammar

Every analytical chart must include, where applicable:

- specific title;
- explanatory subtitle;
- unit;
- sample definition;
- period;
- source/publisher;
- retrieval date;
- transformation note;
- evidence status;
- material limitation.

Charts are generated from code for reproducible releases. Decorative visualizations and untraceable infographic numbers are not accepted as evidence objects.

## Tables

Tables identify unit, denominator, sample, period, missingness treatment and source perimeter where material. Significant figures must reflect measurement precision rather than visual convenience.

## Covers and identifiers

Every formal publication carries its institutional identifier, title, version/date, evidence state and suggested citation where citable. Filenames use stable identifiers rather than `FINAL_v3` naming.

## Evidence labels

Evidence labels describe the evidentiary origin or validation state of a claim. They are not decorative trust badges. `EXTERNAL REVIEW` and `INDEPENDENT REPRODUCTION` may appear only when the corresponding registry record exists.
