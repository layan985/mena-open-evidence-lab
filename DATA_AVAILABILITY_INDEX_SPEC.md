# MENA Data Availability Index — Measurement Specification

_Last updated: 16 August 2026_

## Purpose

The index measures observable publication infrastructure. It does **not** score the competence, integrity or independence of a national statistical agency.

## Dimensions

1. **Timeliness** — median publication delay from reference period to release.
2. **Historical availability** — years of retrievable historical observations.
3. **Machine readability** — presence and quality of structured downloads.
4. **Vintage preservation** — whether prior releases remain retrievable.
5. **Metadata** — availability of definitions and methodology.
6. **Revision transparency** — whether revisions are explicitly identified and documented.
7. **Citation permanence** — stability and persistence of source URLs or identifiers.
8. **Granularity** — availability of geographic, demographic or sector breakdowns where relevant.
9. **Reproducibility** — whether published inputs are sufficient to reconstruct a reported statistic.

## Scoring rules

- Every component score must be decomposable to observable measurements.
- Missing evidence is represented as missing, not silently scored zero.
- Country totals cannot be published without the component table.
- Weighting choices must be versioned and accompanied by a sensitivity analysis.
- Rank changes caused by methodology revisions must be distinguished from changes in source infrastructure.
- No subjective overall score may be introduced without a documented coding protocol and second review.

## Minimum release package

A release requires the country-component table, definitions, source register, retrieval dates, scoring code, missingness report, weighting specification, sensitivity analysis, QA report, limitations register, version identifier and correction route.

## Required disclaimer

> The index measures observable publication infrastructure, not the competence or independence of national statistical agencies.
