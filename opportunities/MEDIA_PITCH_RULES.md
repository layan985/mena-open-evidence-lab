# Media Pitch Rules

## What merits a pitch

Pitch only when the Lab has:

- a verified finding that changes how a familiar statistic should be interpreted;
- a dataset or source package the journalist can inspect;
- a clear distinction between the empirical fact and the broader implication;
- a named limitation that prevents overclaiming.

## Pitch anatomy

1. One sentence with the actual finding.
2. One sentence explaining why a reader would care.
3. One sentence explaining the source/method.
4. Offer the source files/data and an on-record explanation.

Do not attach a generic institutional deck.

## Best beats

- economics and macro policy;
- data journalism;
- Gulf/MENA business and economics;
- official statistics and data governance;
- research methods/reproducibility.

## Do not say

- 'the official numbers were fake';
- 'the government changed history';
- 'we discovered the true GDP';
- 'unprecedented' unless tested against a defined comparison set;
- 'first ever' without a defensible literature/search basis.

## Better framing

The story is not that revision is suspicious. The story is that historical analysis needs historical information sets, and those information sets are often harder to preserve and reconstruct than users realize.
