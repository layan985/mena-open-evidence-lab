# MENA Evidence Lab — Targeted Outreach Sequence

## Rule

No broad 'would love to collaborate' messages. Every contact gets:

1. one concrete research object or finding;
2. one reason it intersects with their remit;
3. one small ask they can answer;
4. one link, not a deck dump.

## Sequence A — Technical validators

Target: official-statistics researchers, data-quality specialists, national-accounts economists, reproducibility researchers.

Ask: independently check one randomly selected source pair or critique one protocol decision.

Success: a documented external check, disagreement or correction.

## Sequence B — Statistical infrastructure organizations

Target: PARIS21, GPSDD, Open Data Watch, UNSD/UNWDF, ESCWA statistical teams.

Lead: the historical-information-set problem, not the Lab biography.

Ask: point to the relevant technical team; suggest an archive/revision standard we should align with; or review the two-axis measurement framework.

Success: technical routing, reviewer, network inclusion or pilot discussion.

## Sequence C — National statistical offices

Target: GASTAT, Jordan DOS, HCP Morocco, INS Tunisia and later additional MENA NSOs.

Lead: an exact missing release or documented methodology transition.

Ask: provide/locate archived files, revision metadata or technical documentation; optionally verify the reconstructed release chain.

Success: primary-source recovery or technical correction.

## Sequence D — Buyers

Target: macro consultancies, bank economics/research teams, forecasting/model teams, development/evaluation contractors.

Lead: one business risk: historical backtests or conclusions may be using data that was unavailable at the evaluation date.

Ask: give the Lab one historical cutoff + country + indicator/model to test in a bounded paid pilot.

Success: scoped diagnostic call or paid reconstruction/audit.

## Sequence E — Data journalists

Lead: one verified narrative-reversal or comparability result plus the underlying source package.

Ask: none in the first message beyond 'happy to provide the release chain or reproduce the calculation.'

Success: substantive citation, coverage or source use, not promotional mention.

## Follow-up cadence

- First follow-up: 5–7 business days if no response and the opportunity is not deadline-driven.
- Deadline-driven tender teaming: follow up after 2 business days, then stop after one additional concise follow-up.
- Technical reviewers: one follow-up with a smaller ask (for example, one source pair instead of a whole protocol).
- Never send repeated generic nudges.

## Message test

Before sending, delete any sentence that could have been sent unchanged by 500 other data organizations.
