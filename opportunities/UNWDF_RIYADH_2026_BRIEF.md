# UN World Data Forum 2026 — MENA Evidence Lab Execution Brief

**Riyadh, Saudi Arabia · 9–12 November 2026**  
**Registration deadline:** 1 October 2026

## Objective

Do not attend as a generic early-stage research organization seeking connections. Attend with one inspectable empirical object and one concrete collaboration request.

## Flagship release

### When the Past Changes: A First Audit of Statistical Vintages in MENA

Minimum release package before travel:

- Saudi 2023 GDP sign-reversal case with complete source manifest;
- three additional primary-source cases, selected for evidence quality rather than surprise;
- Measurement Stability Framework;
- Archive Reconstructability Grades for all included cases;
- machine-readable observations and source hashes;
- reproducible calculation code;
- limitations/missing-vintage register;
- two-page policy/institutional brief;
- one-page technical collaboration note.

## The 20-second explanation

MENA Evidence Lab reconstructs the official information set that existed at a historical date. We preserve statistical vintages, methodology changes and source files, then test whether economic conclusions survive later revisions. We are building the regional evidence infrastructure needed to distinguish what was known then from what is known now.

## The finding to lead with

Use the Saudi 2023 case only as an entry point, with a strict claim boundary:

> The final pre-comprehensive-revision official vintage described 2023 real GDP growth as −0.8%; the revised national accounts report +0.5%. The economic year did not change after the fact. The measurement system did. The Lab preserves both information sets and the documented transition between them.

Do not imply error, manipulation or institutional failure.

## Registration text

### Organization purpose
MENA Evidence Lab is an independent research initiative building auditable infrastructure for regional economic evidence. The Lab preserves official statistical vintages, methodology changes and source provenance, and develops reproducible methods for testing how revisions affect economic analysis and public claims.

### Reason for attending
I would like to contribute a regional measurement-economics perspective to discussions on official-statistics quality, trust, reproducibility and data infrastructure. The Lab is currently reconstructing historical MENA statistical vintages and developing an open protocol for measuring revision exposure and archive recoverability. I am particularly interested in technical exchange with statistical offices and organizations working on historical data preservation, revision policy and reproducible public evidence.

### Additional programme role / contribution
If useful to the programme, I would be glad to contribute a short empirical case or technical demonstration on reconstructing historical official-data information sets and testing whether published economic conclusions survive later revisions. The work is source-linked and designed for independent reproduction.

- primary-source access that unlocks a new country reconstruction;
- an external technical reviewer;
