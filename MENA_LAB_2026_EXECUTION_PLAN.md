# MENA Evidence Lab — Execution Plan

_Last updated: 22 August 2026_

## Institutional thesis

MENA Evidence Lab should become the region's independent audit layer for economic evidence: preserving what official statistics said at each point in time, documenting changes in definitions and methods, and testing whether downstream claims survive those changes.

The Lab compounds four assets:

1. **Public evidence infrastructure** that creates trust and citations.
2. **Original measurement-economics research** that creates intellectual distinction.
3. **Bounded commissioned work** that converts the same methods into revenue.
4. **Institutional distribution** through statistical, development, research and media networks.

## Flagship objective: Riyadh 2026

Target: arrive at the UN World Data Forum, 9–12 November 2026, with a released empirical object rather than a generic institutional pitch.

### Release package

Working title: **When the Past Changes: A First Audit of Statistical Vintages in MENA**

Minimum credible release:

- Saudi Arabia complete reconstruction around the 2023 GDP narrative reversal;
- three additional documented methodology-shock or revision cases where primary sources permit;
- Measurement Stability Framework methodology note;
- archive-reconstructability grades for included cases;
- machine-readable observation manifest;
- reproducible calculations;
- limitations and missing-vintage register;
- one two-page institutional brief;
- one press/data-journalist brief;
- one commercial note explaining applications to historical model evaluation.

No regional ranking is published unless the pre-declared sample gates are met.

## Workstream B — Claim Survival Project

### Pilot

- 100 pre-registered claims.
- Four equal source strata: official; multilateral/development; financial/economic research; major media/public policy.
- Initial countries: Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Morocco, Tunisia, conditional on vintage recoverability.
- Independently double-code at least 20%.
- Release reversals and survivals; do not optimize the sample for surprising failures.

### Primary public result

The interesting quantity is not a gallery of mistakes. It is the empirical probability that a dated economic conclusion remains true under later comparable official vintages, together with the conditions under which it fails.

## Workstream C — Revenue

The Lab sells bounded applications of methods already visible in public.

### Priority offer 1 — Historical Information-Set Reconstruction

Buyer: economists, banks, investment research teams, consultancies, litigators and evaluators.

Deliverable: exact official information set observable at a specified historical cutoff, source archive, release chronology, transformations and limitations.

Price test: US$1,500–5,000 for a bounded country/indicator reconstruction.

### Priority offer 2 — Statistical Revision / Look-Ahead Audit

Buyer: forecasting teams, macro model owners, investment/research teams and academics.

Deliverable: test whether latest-vintage data contaminated historical evaluation, quantify conclusion changes under real-time vintages and separate routine revisions from methodology shocks.

Price test: US$2,500–7,500 bounded scope.

### Priority offer 3 — Indicator & Provenance Audit

Buyer: development consultancies, NGOs, multilaterals and research programs.

Deliverable: definitions, denominator integrity, provenance, comparability, versioning and claim-level audit.

Price test: US$5,000–12,000.

### Priority offer 4 — Dataset Build / Replication Audit

Buyer: research organizations, universities, evaluation firms and institutional research teams.

Price test: US$5,000–25,000 depending on perimeter.

Prices are positioning tests, not promises of market willingness to pay. Track conversion and revise from evidence.

## Workstream E — Distribution

### Institutional target classes

- official statistics: GASTAT, Jordan DOS, HCP Morocco, INS Tunisia, GCC-STAT;
- regional/international statistical infrastructure: ESCWA, UNSD, PARIS21, World Bank Data, IMF statistics/data teams;
- data-for-development networks: GPSDD, Open Data Watch, Development Gateway;
- research/economic users: regional macro consultancies, forecasting teams, economic research units;
- public distribution: data journalists, economics correspondents, reproducibility researchers.

Every approach should carry one falsifiable result or one inspectable research object. No generic 'partnership' begging.

## Workstream F — Publication engine

The Lab publishes from evidence objects, not content calendars.

One research object should generate:

- source/data release;
- methods note;
- evidence note;
- one headline chart/table;
- one public post;
- one institutional outreach message;
- one buyer-facing use case;
- one request for independent validation.

This converts research effort into citations, discovery, validation and leads without manufacturing separate content.

## Metrics that matter

Track monthly:

- verified vintage observations added;
- historical releases recovered;
- archive gaps documented;
- independently checked claims/cases;
- external dataset downloads/uses;
- citations or substantive external references;
- qualified institutional conversations;
- proposals submitted;
- paid commissioned engagements;
- revenue;
- repeat buyers;
- corrections issued and closed.

Do not optimize follower counts independently of evidence use, qualified inbound or citation.

## Non-negotiable claim discipline

The Lab does not imply that a revision is an error, that a methodological change is manipulation, or that a narrative reversal means the underlying economy changed retrospectively. Surprise is not a substitute for a valid comparison.

The strongest brand position is that the Lab will publish an inconvenient null result as readily as a spectacular reversal.
