# MODERN Undergraduate Research Fellowship — Cohort 01

MODERN is the contributor and research network of the **MENA Open Data & Evidence Lab**. The fellowship is a selective production track inside MODERN; it is not a membership credential.

## Cohort size

**11 fellows.** Productive contribution quality is more important than applicant volume.

## Public calendar — 2026

- **August 10** — public announcement
- **August 11–22** — applications open + technical task
- **August 23–25** — shortlist review
- **August 27** — founding cohort announced
- **August 29** — orientation
- **September 1** — first production sprint

## Selection criteria

Applications are scored on evidence, not prestige.

| Dimension | Weight | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Data/research execution | 30% | Can clean, structure, document or analyze messy evidence |
| Reproducibility discipline | 25% | Clear provenance, deterministic work, transparent limitations |
| Technical task quality | 25% | Correctness, judgment, documentation and reviewability |
| Research reasoning | 10% | Can identify definitions, threats, ambiguity and methodological choices |
| Reliability and contribution fit | 10% | Scope discipline, communication and ability to ship |

University name, follower count, certificates and polished branding are not substitutes for work quality.

## Required technical task

Applicants receive a deliberately messy mini evidence package containing:

1. two small tabular source files with inconsistent entity/date fields;
2. one source note or public-document extract;
3. duplicated and ambiguous observations;
4. at least one licensing/provenance question.

Applicants must submit:

- a cleaned machine-readable output;
- a short README describing every material transformation;
- a source/provenance table;
- a reproducible script or notebook;
- a 250-word research note identifying at least two unresolved risks or ambiguities.

**Evaluation rule:** do not reward applicants for silently guessing. Flagging an ambiguity can score higher than inventing a clean answer.

## Completion standard

A fellow graduates only after completing **all four** requirements:

1. Attend or complete materials for at least 5 of 6 core workshops.
2. Make at least one substantive, reviewable contribution to a public dataset release that passes Lab validation.
3. Complete one replication or reproducibility audit of another team's work.
4. Submit a final research memo documenting methods, contribution, limitations, and lessons.

Attendance alone is insufficient. Certificates must state exact completed requirements and must not imply university accreditation unless it exists.

## Curriculum

Git/GitHub; reproducible cleaning; Python/R; SQL/DuckDB; provenance; entity resolution; panel construction; regression; difference-in-differences; event studies; visualization; replication; DOI-backed publishing.

## Credit

Credit follows verified contribution. Fellows should receive release-level authorship or contributor credit appropriate to what they actually shipped, with ORCID-compatible names/identifiers collected when available. No credit is issued for attendance alone.
