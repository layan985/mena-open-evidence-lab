When the Economy Changes Without the Economy Changing

What happens downstream when a national statistical office changes the measurement system for GDP? Four MENA cases show four different consequences: a growth narrative can reverse sign, fiscal ratios can move because their denominator changed, the measured structure of an economy can be rewritten, and an international income classification can cross a formal threshold.

The object of interest is not revision magnitude alone. The research object is the propagation chain: measurement-system change → revised level/growth/composition → derived ratio or threshold → narrative or institutional consequence.
Case 01 · Narrative/sign propagation

Saudi Arabia: the same 2023 changes from contraction to growth.

GASTAT's comprehensive update raised the 2023 nominal GDP estimate by 14.1%, equivalent to SAR 566 billion, and reported the non-oil share at 53.2%, 5.7 percentage points above the earlier estimate. The final pre-comprehensive-revision annual growth estimate preserved in the 2024 Q4 release was −0.8%. The revised annual accounts report +0.5%.

This is a change in the official statistical description of 2023. It does not mean the underlying economy changed after 2023 ended.

Case 02 · Denominator propagation

Tunisia: GDP gets bigger; the debt ratio gets smaller.

INS replaced the 1997-base accounts with a 2015-base system using SNA 2008 and expanded source coverage. The 2015 GDP level rose by 6%, from an implied TND 84.6892bn to TND 89.8022bn. Because GDP is the denominator of many headline ratios, the historical fiscal picture also changes mechanically. For 2020, public debt falls from 88.6% to 83.5% of GDP and the budget deficit from 11.8% to 11.1%.

The useful counterpoint is growth: despite the 6% level shift, INS reports average annual real growth of 0.8% under both bases for 2010–2020. Individual years still move: 2019 rises from 0.9% to 1.4%, while 2020 falls from −8.8% to −9.2%.

Case 03 · Composition propagation

Morocco: a larger measured economy does not mean uniformly higher growth.

HCP's move from base 2007 to base 2014 raised 2014 nominal GDP from MAD 925.376bn to MAD 1,001.454bn, an 8.2% increase. The measured structure changed too: the tertiary-sector share rose from 51.6% to 54.8%.

But the annual growth profile moves in both directions. HCP reports 2016 growth falling from 1.1% to 0.5%, while 2017 rises from 4.2% to 5.1%. Average annual growth over 2014–2019 changes only from 3.1% to 3.2%. This is why level revisions and growth revisions must be treated as separate objects.

Case 04 · Institutional-threshold propagation

Jordan: a statistical revision helped change the country's World Bank income category.

The World Bank's FY27 classification note states that Jordan's comprehensive national-accounts revision found the economy to be nearly 10% larger than previously estimated. Together with 2.8% growth in 2025, the revision pushed Jordan from lower-middle to upper-middle income.

The classification arithmetic is unusually visible. Jordan's 2024 Atlas-method GNI per capita was US$4,430, US$66 below the then upper-middle threshold of US$4,496. For FY27, the threshold is US$4,636 and Jordan's 2025 Atlas GNI per capita is US$5,260: US$624 above it.

The US$830 increase in Atlas GNI per capita cannot be attributed entirely to rebasing. Growth, population, exchange-rate smoothing and the Atlas method also matter. The defensible claim is narrower: the World Bank explicitly identifies the comprehensive revision as a decisive contributor to the threshold crossing.

What this release does not claim

  • These four cases are selected for distinct propagation mechanisms. They are not a representative sample of MENA revisions.
  • No regional average revision magnitude is estimated.
  • A large revision is not scored as incompetence, manipulation or poor statistical quality.
  • Comprehensive methodology changes are separated from routine revisions.
  • Ratio changes caused by a new GDP denominator are not described as equivalent changes in the underlying nominal numerator.
  • Jordan's income-classification move is not attributed entirely to national-accounts rebasing.

Next test

The next release expands from four forensic cases to a pre-registered episode universe. Each rebasing episode will be coded for the GDP-level shock, annual-growth revisions, composition revisions, GDP-denominated ratios, per-capita measures, rankings or thresholds, methodological drivers and archive reconstructability. Only after the universe and inclusion rules are frozen will the Lab estimate prevalence.
