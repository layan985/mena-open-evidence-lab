# Commit to Data — Draft Submission Record

## Commitment title

MENA Statistical Memory Project: Preserve What Official Data Said, When It Said It

## Sponsoring organization

MENA Open Data & Evidence Lab

## Type of organization

Civil society / academia-adjacent independent research organization

## Data focus

Official statistics; administrative data; monitoring and evaluation; data for SDG monitoring; open data; statistical metadata and revision history.

## Commitment

Between August 2026 and November 2027, MENA Open Data & Evidence Lab commits to building an open, source-linked statistical memory layer for selected official economic statistics across at least 10 MENA economies.

The project will preserve release-level evidence that is commonly lost when statistical websites overwrite files, revise historical values, change classifications or update methodologies. Rather than storing only the latest number, each record will preserve what an official statistic said at a particular release date, its population and concept, its methodology regime, source location, revision state and relationship to later vintages.

## Measurable outputs

By November 2027 the Lab aims to publish:

1. at least 500 release-level statistical-vintage records across at least 10 MENA economies;
2. a public methodology-change registry covering rebasing, classification changes, survey redesigns and material data-source changes in the selected series;
3. an open statistical comparability schema that records when similarly labelled indicators are directly comparable, comparable only with adjustment, or not directly comparable;
4. a public correction and change ledger for released records;
5. machine-readable downloads and source registers for all releasable records;
6. at least one independent external review or reproduction of the methodology and one published response to that review;
7. a short public methods report describing what is lost when only latest-vintage data are retained.

## Why it matters

Official statistics are revised because measurement improves. The problem is not revision itself. The problem for reproducible research is that earlier information states can become difficult or impossible to reconstruct. That makes it harder to know what policymakers, researchers and forecasters could actually have known at a historical date and can create accidental hindsight in retrospective evaluation.

The MENA Statistical Memory Project treats release time, vintage, definitions and methodology as part of the data rather than disposable metadata.

## Existing foundation

The Lab has already released a Statistical Vintage Register, Vintage Vault, real-time-data protocol, Methodology Shock Registry, and a pilot Statistical Comparability Graph. A verified Saudi Arabia national-accounts case demonstrates the problem: the official recorded 2023 real GDP growth rate moved from -0.8% in a pre-comprehensive-revision vintage to +0.5% after the comprehensive revision, changing the qualitative description of the year from contraction to growth.

## Progress reporting

Progress will be reported publicly through versioned releases, change logs, correction records and periodic counts of preserved vintages, countries, method changes and external validations.

## Collaboration sought

The Lab welcomes methodological review, archival collaboration, source corrections, replication, and partnerships with national statistical offices, researchers and organizations working on open and trusted official statistics.
