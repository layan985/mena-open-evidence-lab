# One-page concept note

## From AI Disclosure to Labour Adjustment: A Source-Verifiable Firm Panel for SWANA

### Research problem

Firm-level discussion of AI in SWANA is expanding faster than the evidence needed to distinguish announcement from operational adoption. Public claims frequently mix generic AI language, named use cases, deployed workflows and measurable organizational effects. That makes it difficult to answer a basic labour-market question: **when firms say they are adopting AI, what observable change has actually occurred, and when does it reach work organization, skills demand or employment?**

### Proposed research

MENA Open Data & Evidence Lab proposes a source-verifiable panel of firms operating across selected SWANA economies. The panel would code AI evidence using an ordered adoption ladder:

0. no qualifying public evidence identified;
1. generic AI disclosure;
2. specific named use case;
3. operational deployment;
4. documented organizational or workforce adjustment;
5. measurable operational or labour outcome.

Each coded event would retain the underlying source, disclosure date, workflow, adoption state, workforce evidence, source type and claim boundary. Employment observations would be versioned where company definitions change. Public disclosure would be treated as a source-bounded evidence system rather than a census of true adoption.

### Research questions

1. How large is the gap between AI disclosure and evidence of operational deployment across SWANA firms?
2. Which sectors progress from generic disclosure to operational use fastest?
3. Does deployment precede observable changes in role design, hiring mix, training, occupational composition or employment?
4. How sensitive are measured adoption patterns to the type of source used?

### Existing foundation

The Lab has an active 50-firm AI and labour research panel with 176 numeric employment firm-year observations collected, 168 currently bound to source hashes, and a public coding schema. The panel remains explicitly pre-freeze: no preferred causal estimate is treated as a released finding while collection and independent coding are incomplete.

### Proposed outputs

- audited firm-event and employment panel;
- AI adoption ladder and codebook;
- descriptive research note on disclosure-to-deployment gaps;
- labour-adjustment analysis where evidence permits;
- source and limitations register;
- reproducible methods package;
- policy brief on what public firm evidence can and cannot establish about AI-driven labour adjustment in SWANA.

### Collaboration sought

The Lab is seeking methodological feedback and, if the concept falls within the Hub's unsolicited-proposal pathway, consideration for development into a full research proposal. A collaboration could also begin more narrowly with external review of the adoption ontology and country/sector sampling design before any funding decision.
