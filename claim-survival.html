Do economic claims survive the data?

The Claim Survival Project reconstructs the official information available when a dated economic claim was made, then asks whether the qualitative conclusion remains true under a later comparable official vintage.

This is not a hunt for mistakes. Claims are sampled before outcome coding. Methodology shocks are separated from routine revisions. Cases that lose comparability are coded as such rather than forced into a reversal result.

What counts as survival?

Different claims require different tests; the project does not reduce every conclusion to one arbitrary score.

Direction

Growth stays growth?

Tests sign and directional claims such as contraction/growth or rising/falling.

Threshold

Still across the line?

Tests claims built around a declared policy or analytical threshold.

Ranking

Does the ordering survive?

Tests country, sector or category comparisons under later comparable vintages.

Magnitude

Same economic class?

Tests pre-registered magnitude bins without pretending tiny numeric movement equals a narrative change.

Why this matters

Retrospective analysis can quietly use revised information that did not exist when an original decision or claim was made. A real-time reconstruction makes that hindsight visible.

If almost every claim survives, that strengthens confidence in the historical narrative. If some fail, the distribution of failures tells us where revisions or measurement changes materially alter what later researchers think people knew.
