How unstable is a published number — and can we recover what people actually knew?

The Lab studies two different problems separately: how official estimates change across comparable vintages, and whether the historical information set can still be independently reconstructed. Methodology shocks are recorded apart from routine revisions.

Axis 01

Axis 02

Archive Reconstructability Grade

Can an independent researcher retrieve the original release, verify its date and value, recover applicable methodology metadata, preserve the source and reproduce the extraction?

A · 6/6 conditions
B · 5/6 with value/date intact
C · 3–4/6 or material ambiguity
D · historical evidence materially unrecoverable

No premature league tables. Quarterly series need at least 12 comparable periods and annual series at least 8, with two observed vintages per period, disclosed missingness, separated methodology shocks, reproducible code and at least 80% source-resolution coverage before ranked results are published.

Why not one clever score?

A large, well-documented revision is not the same problem as a missing historical record. A rebasing exercise is not the same event as an ordinary revision. Collapsing them into one number would hide the mechanism the research is trying to understand.
