What counts as a methodology shock?

The taxonomy is broad because measurement breaks do not arrive under one standardized label.

base_year_change

Rebasing or benchmark-year changes.

classification_change

ISIC, COICOP or other classification migration.

chain_linking_adoption

Changes to volume-measure construction.

new_census_incorporation

Population or economic census benchmarks enter estimates.

administrative_data_adoption

Tax, registry, payments or other admin sources enter production.

survey_redesign

Questionnaire, sample or collection-system changes.

seasonal_adjustment_change

Model or calendar-treatment changes.

population_rebasing

Revised population controls affect levels and denominators.

sample_redesign

Sampling-frame changes capable of moving a published series.

Econometric consequence

Measurement error can change its process at a known date.

Yobs(t) = Y*(t) + m(t)

If the measurement process changes at T, m(t) is not necessarily harmless stationary noise. A methodology shock can affect trends, break detection, cross-country comparisons, forecast evaluation and causal estimates. The registry exists so researchers can model or stress-test that break explicitly.
