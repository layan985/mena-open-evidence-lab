Revision Debt

A framework for studying how long preliminary economic knowledge remains authoritative, how far it later moves, and whether the revision changes the story people were entitled to tell at the time.

Specification →First case →

Three components. No fake precision.

Version 0.1 does not collapse the construct into a single weighted score.

01
Magnitude

How far a later comparable official vintage moves from the earlier value in the indicator's natural unit.

02
Exposure duration

How long the earlier official estimate remained the latest comparable public information.

03
Narrative consequence

Whether the revision flips a sign, ranking, threshold or acceleration claim rather than merely changing a decimal.

First verified case · Saudi Arabia · 2023 real GDP
−0.8%→+0.5%

Comparable revision: +1.3 percentage points. Narrative class: sign reversal, from contraction to growth. The underlying 2023 economy did not change retroactively; the official statistical record did.

Research programme

The next step is not to manufacture a regional leaderboard. It is to collect directly comparable vintage chains across national accounts, prices and labour statistics; distinguish ordinary revisions from methodology breaks; and test which qualitative narratives survive.

Potential paper

The empirical question is whether revision magnitude, elapsed exposure and measurement-system changes predict narrative reversals—and whether conclusions drawn from first releases are systematically more fragile in particular indicators or regimes.

Claim boundary

Revision is normal statistical practice and often improves measurement. Revision Debt measures the time structure of economic knowledge, not institutional competence.
