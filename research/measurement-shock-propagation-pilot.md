# Measurement Shock Propagation — Four-Country Pilot

_Last updated: 22 August 2026_

## Research question

When a national statistical office performs a comprehensive national-accounts revision, where does the change propagate?

This pilot does **not** rank countries and does not treat rebasing as error. It classifies downstream consequences of documented measurement-system changes using official statistical sources and closely linked institutional sources.

## Preliminary result

Across four MENA cases, the same broad statistical event — a comprehensive national-accounts revision — propagated through four different objects:

1. **Saudi Arabia: narrative/sign propagation.** The 2023 real-GDP description changes from contraction in the pre-revision vintage to growth in the revised accounts, while the nominal 2023 GDP level is revised upward by 14.1%.
2. **Tunisia: denominator/ratio propagation.** The 2015 GDP level is revised upward by 6%; the larger denominator mechanically lowers headline fiscal ratios, including 2020 public debt from 88.6% to 83.5% of GDP.
3. **Jordan: institutional-threshold propagation.** The World Bank states that Jordan's comprehensive national-accounts revision found the economy to be nearly 10% larger than previously estimated; together with 2025 growth, the revision pushed Jordan from lower-middle to upper-middle income in the FY27 classification.
4. **Morocco: composition/growth-profile propagation.** The 2014 nominal GDP level is revised upward by 8.2%, while the tertiary-sector share rises from 51.6% to 54.8% of GDP and individual annual real-growth rates move in both directions.

The core empirical object is therefore not simply `revision magnitude`. It is a **propagation chain**:

`measurement-system change -> revised level/growth/composition -> derived ratio or threshold -> narrative/institutional consequence`

## Case 1 — Saudi Arabia

### Documented measurement change

GASTAT's comprehensive update raised the 2023 GDP estimate by 14.1%, equivalent to SAR 566 billion. The updated nominal GDP level is approximately SAR 4.57 trillion. GASTAT also reports that the non-oil share of nominal GDP increased to 53.2%, 5.7 percentage points above the earlier estimate.

### Propagation mechanism

The Lab's preserved pre-revision release reports 2023 real GDP growth at -0.8%, while the revised annual national accounts report +0.5%.

**Propagation type:** sign/narrative reversal.

### Claim boundary

This is a change in the official statistical description of 2023, not a claim that economic activity itself changed after the year ended.

### Primary sources

- GASTAT, `Comprehensive update of Saudi Arabia's GDP`, 1 May 2025: https://www.stats.gov.sa/en/w/news/38
- GASTAT, `Annual National Accounts Publication 2023`.

## Case 2 — Tunisia

### Documented measurement change

Statistiques Tunisie replaced the 1997-base national accounts with a 2015-base system aligned with SNA 2008 and using improved source coverage and estimation methods.

For 2015, nominal GDP rises to TND 89.8022 billion, an upward revision of TND 5.113 billion or 6% relative to the old-base estimate.

The revised system also changes the composition of value added: services rise from 43.6% to 46.1% of total value added, while building rises from 4.7% to 5.7%.

### Downstream ratios

The INS explicitly notes that the larger GDP denominator mechanically lowers government ratios. For 2020:

- budget deficit: 11.8% -> 11.1% of GDP (-0.7 pp)
- public debt: 88.6% -> 83.5% of GDP (-5.1 pp)

The investment rate for 2015 rises from 19.8% to 21.0% of GDP.

Real-growth revisions are smaller and bidirectional: 2019 is revised from 0.9% to 1.4%; 2020 from -8.8% to -9.2%.

**Propagation type:** denominator/ratio propagation.

### Primary source

- Institut National de la Statistique, `Les Comptes Nationaux changent de base`, 15 August 2021: https://www.ins.tn/publication/les-comptes-nationaux-changent-de-base

## Case 3 — Jordan

### Documented measurement change

Jordan's Department of Statistics completed a comprehensive revision of the national accounts and now publishes a revised GDP series. The World Bank's July 2026 classification note states that the rebasing exercise found Jordan's economy to be nearly 10% larger than previously estimated, attributing the change to expanded coverage, updated surveys, new data sources and enhanced national-accounts compilation methodology.

### Institutional consequence

On 1 July 2026 the World Bank reclassified Jordan from lower-middle income to upper-middle income for FY27. The Bank states that the national-accounts revision, together with 2.8% growth in 2025, pushed the country clearly across the threshold.

Current World Bank data report 2025 Atlas-method GNI per capita of US$5,260. The FY27 upper-middle threshold is US$4,636, leaving Jordan US$624 above it. The prior classification used a 2024 GNI-per-capita estimate of US$4,430, below the then-applicable US$4,496 threshold.

**Propagation type:** institutional threshold/classification propagation.

### Claim boundary

The increase from the old classification input to the new one cannot be attributed entirely to rebasing: growth, population, exchange-rate smoothing and Atlas-method mechanics also matter. The defensible claim is the World Bank's: the comprehensive revision was a decisive contributor to the reclassification.

### Sources

- Jordan Department of Statistics, Annual Accounts: https://dosweb.dos.gov.jo/nationalaccount/annual-accounts/
- World Bank, `Who moves up and why? A closer look at the 2026-2027 release of the World Bank Group Country Income Classifications`, 1 July 2026.
- World Bank, FY27 Country and Lending Groups / WDI metadata.

## Case 4 — Morocco

### Documented measurement change

The Haut-Commissariat au Plan moved national accounts from base year 2007 to 2014 and incorporated SNA 2008 recommendations and new statistical sources.

For 2014:

- nominal GDP: MAD 925.376bn -> MAD 1,001.454bn (+MAD 76.078bn; +8.2%)
- tertiary value added: MAD 477.7bn -> MAD 548.3bn (+14.8%)
- tertiary share of GDP: 51.6% -> 54.8% (+3.2 pp)
- household final consumption expenditure: +11.2%
- employee compensation: +14.7%

The level revision does not imply uniformly higher growth. HCP reports 2016 growth changing from 1.1% to 0.5% (-0.6 pp) and 2017 from 4.2% to 5.1% (+0.9 pp); average annual growth over 2014-2019 moves only from 3.1% to 3.2%.

**Propagation type:** structural-composition and growth-profile propagation.

### Primary sources

- HCP, `Changement de l'année de base des comptes nationaux 2014 au lieu de 2007`: https://www.hcp.ma/Changement-de-l-annee-de-base-des-comptes-nationaux-2014-au-lieu-de-2007_a3106.html
- HCP, `Comptes nationaux base 2014 (2014-2019)`.

## Cross-case interpretation

These four cases suggest a more useful research taxonomy than a single revision score.

### Level shock

How much does the measured size of the economy move?

Observed documented point estimates in this pilot:

- Tunisia 2015: +6.0%
- Morocco 2014: +8.2%
- Jordan: nearly +10% (World Bank characterization)
- Saudi Arabia 2023: +14.1%

These cases are not a representative sample and should not be averaged into a regional estimate.

### Narrative shock

Does the qualitative description of a period change? Saudi Arabia provides a verified sign reversal.

### Denominator shock

Do ratios that use GDP as a denominator materially change without the numerator representing a new underlying event? Tunisia provides a clear public-debt and fiscal-deficit example.

### Composition shock

Does the measured sector structure change materially? Morocco and Saudi Arabia provide strong examples.

### Institutional-threshold shock

Does revised measurement alter a rule-based external classification? Jordan provides a direct case through the World Bank income classification.

## Next empirical test

The next phase should estimate the **Measurement Shock Propagation Matrix** over a larger pre-registered set of MENA rebasing episodes.

For each episode, record:

- reference year and old/new accounting base;
- nominal GDP level revision;
- growth-profile revisions;
- sector-share revisions;
- GDP-denominated fiscal/debt ratio revisions;
- GDP-per-capita/GNI-per-capita changes;
- threshold or ranking changes;
- source/recoverability grade;
- exact methodological drivers;
- whether the downstream consequence was mechanical, partly mechanical, or institutionally mediated.

No claim about regional prevalence should be made until the episode universe and inclusion rules are frozen.
