When does economic history change its mind?

The Narrative Reversal Registry records cases where a later comparable official statistical vintage changes a qualitative conclusion implied by an earlier one.

A narrative reversal occurs when the same underlying economic object, observed through two comparable official statistical vintages, crosses a decision-relevant qualitative boundary.

Four reversal classes

The registry separates changes in magnitude from changes in meaning.

01

Sign reversal

Contraction becomes growth, or growth becomes contraction.

02

Ranking reversal

The ordering of countries, sectors or entities changes across vintages.

03

Threshold reversal

A revised value crosses a policy, analytical or contractual threshold.

04

Direction reversal

Acceleration becomes deceleration, or the reverse.

Admission rule

A surprising revision is not automatically a narrative reversal.

Required

Same object, comparable vintages

The two values must refer to the same indicator concept, geography and reference period, or a documented methodological bridge must justify comparability.

Required

Official source chain

Both vintages must be traceable to the statistical authority or another primary official release. Search snippets and secondary databases do not establish a record.

Required

Qualitative boundary

The revision must change a sign, ranking, threshold classification or acceleration/deceleration conclusion. Large revisions that do not cross a boundary remain revision events, not narrative reversals.

Required

Claim boundary

The record describes a change in statistical measurement. It does not imply that underlying historical economic activity changed retroactively.
