The economy exists before the statistic does.

MENA-RTD is a real-time data and vintages database designed to reconstruct what an analyst could actually have known on a historical date. It stores releases, not just the latest historical value, and keeps flash estimates, full releases, archive dates and later revisions separate.

Q1 2026 · release architecture
29 → 103 days

The current sourced seed spans 29 days after quarter-end for Saudi Arabia's flash estimate to 103 days for Oman's formal quarterly-national-accounts catalogue publication: a 74-day spread across recorded release objects.

That is not yet a harmonized “first information” estimate. Saudi publishes a flash estimate; Oman has evidence of preliminary figures before the formal catalogue date; Jordan exposes conflicting official dates. Those differences are part of the measurement problem.

Why this matters

Today's panel is cleaner than the historical information set ever was.

A database downloaded now can place every country's Q1 number neatly beside the others. An analyst standing in May or June 2026 did not have that panel. MENA-RTD reconstructs the missing time dimension: release channel, release date, vintage, revision and method state.

Build statusThe repository currently contains a four-economy, five-release Q1 seed. The 2015–2026 ten-economy, five-indicator archive is an active build target; the planned 5,000-release threshold has not been claimed as complete.

New measurement objects

MENA-RTD is infrastructure for econometrics, not a release calendar with nicer graphics.

01 · information completeness

How much of the regional economy was statistically observable on date d?

C(t,d) = Σ w_c · 1{release(c,t) ≤ d} / Σ w_c

Computed under explicit equal-country, GDP or population weights. The release clock and weight vintage are part of the statistic.

02 · information half-life

T50, T75 and T90

T_p = first days after period-end where C(t,d) ≥ p

This turns statistical capacity into an interpretable time measure: how long until 50%, 75% or 90% of the chosen regional weight is observable?

03 · revision risk

First estimates are estimates.

R = Y(v) − Y(first)

Historical first-to-later revisions can support empirical revision-risk intervals, news-versus-noise tests and ranking uncertainty without pretending the interval is a sampling confidence interval.

Research unlocked by the archive

The archive is being built so models can be evaluated against the information that existed at the time, not against revised history.

Statistical asynchrony

Panel and survival models of release latency by country, indicator, capacity and release architecture.

Speed versus revision

Does publishing earlier predict larger later revisions, or do stronger statistical systems achieve both speed and accuracy?

Pseudo-real-time nowcasting

Dynamic-factor, state-space and alternative-data models scored only using vintages that actually existed at each historical date.

Technical standard

Try to kill the finding before publishing it.

Serious results require frozen raw data, provenance, hashes, versioned transformations, missingness analysis, sensitivity analysis, effect sizes and uncertainty, out-of-sample validation where relevant, negative results and a replication package. Every material conclusion must survive at least one deliberately hostile but defensible specification.
