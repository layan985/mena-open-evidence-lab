Research architecture · five programmes

Research built around evidence that can survive a changing measurement system.

The Lab’s projects are organized around five recurring research programmes. Measurement economics connects them: release timing, vintages, definitions, methodology changes, physical evidence and uncertainty are treated as part of the empirical object rather than metadata added at the end.

Programme 02

Alternative Economic Measurement

Use physical and high-frequency signals as independent noisy measurements of latent activity, then study disagreement with official first releases rather than automatically calling one side wrong.

Latent activity stateOfficial–physical divergenceMeasurement dispersion
Research direction. Test whether high dispersion across measurement systems predicts larger subsequent official revisions. If it does, alternative data becomes a predictor of measurement uncertainty, not just another GDP forecast.
Programme 03

Firm Dynamics

Build source-bounded observable firm panels and probabilistic activity states to study hiring, technology adoption, procurement and survival without pretending public evidence equals a census.
