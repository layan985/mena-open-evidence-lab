Saudi Arabia Didn't Become 14% Larger Overnight

What changed when GASTAT comprehensively revised the 2023 national accounts—and why an official source is not enough unless the statistical vintage travels with it.

2023 Saudi GDP · previous estimate
↓

Earlier national-accounts vintage

Comprehensive update announced 1 May 2025
+14.1%
+SAR 566bn

GASTAT reported that the updated 2023 GDP estimate reached approximately SAR 4.5 trillion.

This is not 14.1% economic growth. It is a revision to the estimated level of 2023 GDP at current prices. The economy did not suddenly produce another SAR 566 billion; its statistical measurement changed.

Finding 01 · Structure changed too

The revision changed the measured composition of the economy.

Finding 02 · What changed in the measurement system?

A larger evidence base fed a more detailed national-accounts system.

New survey inputsComprehensive Economic Survey; Household Income & Expenditure Survey; Comprehensive Agricultural Survey.
Administrative evidenceExpanded use of administrative data alongside new and richer statistical sources.
Balancing frameworkMore detailed supply-and-use tables supporting production, income and expenditure estimates.
Classification depth134 economic activities in the update, compared with 85 previously.

GASTAT also adopted chain-linked methodology for real GDP measurement. That is a related national-accounts improvement, but this note does not treat chain-linking as the explanation for the 14.1% revision to the current-price 2023 GDP level.

Finding 03 · The research problem
SOURCE ≠ VINTAGE

Two researchers can cite the same official institution and still be working with different economic histories.

GASTAT's comprehensive-revision FAQ says historical GDP estimates were recalibrated through a backcasting approach and warns users not to mix previous and revised vintages in economic modelling. A paper assembled before the comprehensive revision can therefore contain a different historical series from a reproduction using today's downloads.

Ratios

Debt/GDP, fiscal ratios and other denominators can move when the GDP level moves.

Structure

Sector shares and the measured non-oil composition can tell a different historical story.

Models

Coefficients, productivity estimates and fitted relationships can change when the underlying series changes.

Comparisons

Cross-country rankings and historical narratives can depend on which vintage is used.

Interpretation boundary

This note documents a statistical revision. It does not describe the earlier estimate as an error, does not recast the 14.1% revision as economic growth, and does not infer a causal explanation for individual sector revisions beyond the methodological changes documented by GASTAT.
