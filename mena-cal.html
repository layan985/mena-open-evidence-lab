MENA-CAL

Open calendar-adjustment infrastructure for MENA economic data. The region’s economic behaviour is generated by Gregorian time and moving Hijri-calendar events simultaneously; the dataset is designed to make Ramadan, Eid, Hajj, working days and historically changing weekend regimes explicit model inputs.

Why historical versioning matters

A calendar variable can be wrong even when the holiday date is right if the working-week regime applied to that date is wrong.

UAE
2022

A working-week reform changes the regressor itself.

The UAE federal working-week system moved from a Friday–Saturday weekend toward a Saturday–Sunday weekend with a Friday half-day from January 2022. MENA-CAL stores the regime as time-varying rather than pretending one weekend definition holds across history.

Methodology

Official seasonal adjustment already recognizes moving religious-calendar effects.

GASTAT documents specialized treatment for Ramadan and Hajj in its seasonal-adjustment methodology. The research question is therefore not whether calendar effects exist, but how much moving-holiday structure remains across published series and statistical systems.

Jordan
Prior evidence

Residual Eid seasonality has been documented before.

An IMF study of Jordan reported residual seasonality associated with Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha after ordinary Gregorian X-12 adjustment. MENA-CAL turns that issue into reusable regional infrastructure rather than a one-off adjustment.

Empirical programme

Does Ramadan survive seasonal adjustment?

Take officially published seasonally adjusted monthly series and test whether statistically detectable Hijri-calendar structure remains after the official adjustment. The result is a residual-calendar audit, not an accusation that an agency “got seasonality wrong.”

Regressors

Moving-holiday exposure

Ramadan share, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, Hajj and working-day controls.

Diagnostics

Lunar-frequency residuals

regARIMA, Fourier terms, dynamic harmonic regression, state-space effects and spectral diagnostics where appropriate.

Evaluation

Out-of-sample loss

Compare baseline forecasts with MENA-CAL-augmented models rather than declaring improvement from in-sample fit alone.
