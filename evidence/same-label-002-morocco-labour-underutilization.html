Same Label, Different Statistic · 002 · 22 Aug 2026

Morocco's labour market can be 10.8% and 22.5% at the same time.

The two numbers answer different questions under Morocco's 2026 employment-survey framework.

Strict unemployment10.8%EMO 2026 · Q1 framework
Composite labour underutilization22.5%EMO 2026 · broader unmet labour demand

The broader measure includes strict unemployment, time-related underemployment and the potential labour force. Among people aged 15–24, the composite rate reaches 45.3%.

Claim boundary. 10.8% and 22.5% are not competing estimates of one quantity. Treating the difference as statistical disagreement would be a category error.

What the second number sees

A strict unemployment rate cannot describe every person who wants or could supply more work. The broader composite measure expands the object to capture additional forms of labour underutilization. Its larger value is therefore partly definitional by construction.

Why this matters for regional comparison

A cross-country chart can silently become invalid when one country's headline is strict unemployment and another figure captures a broader labour-underutilization concept. The Lab's Comparability Graph therefore stores concept and population as part of the observation, not as a footnote.
