Method Break 002 · Jordan · National accounts

Jordan's quarterly GDP history has a method version.

A revised historical series can look continuous while the statistical system underneath it has changed.

Finding

Jordan's Department of Statistics states that quarterly GDP estimates from 2008 through Q2 2025 underwent a comprehensive revision, alongside adoption of ISIC Rev.4 and an expansion of sector detail from 14 to 20 activities.

What this changes for researchers

A historical quarterly GDP value retrieved today may not be the same statistical vintage that appeared in an older report, forecast, model or policy document. Sector definitions may also differ from archived pre-revision material.

Research-use rule: preserve the release vintage and method version when reconstructing historical analysis. Do not splice archived pre-revision sector series into the revised series without an explicit bridge and documented mapping.

Why this matters

Backcasting improves continuity for current analysis, but it can erase the information state that existed when earlier decisions were made. Current-history analysis and real-time-history analysis are different tasks and should use different vintage logic.

Claim boundary

This note does not infer that the comprehensive revision made Jordan's GDP statistics less reliable. Comprehensive revisions are normal features of national accounts. The note records the identity change that matters for reproducibility and historical reconstruction.
