Which Saudi unemployment rate are you comparing?

The formula is not the problem. The target population is. Q1 2026 unemployment figures can carry the same headline label while representing different nationality scopes.

Core finding

A statistic can be correctly calculated and still be incorrectly compared.

GASTAT reports 6.4% unemployment among Saudis in Q1 2026 and 3.1% for the total population. Jordan's Department of Statistics reports 16.1% for the total population in Jordan — explicitly Jordanians and non-Jordanians. Putting Saudi 6.4% beside Jordan 16.1% therefore mixes nationality scopes before any substantive interpretation begins.

Same label. Different population.

The published values are legitimate. The naive comparison is not.

Why this matters

Data pipelines routinely preserve country, date and value while dropping the metadata that determines what a value means. That creates a quiet failure mode in cross-country dashboards, consultant models, newsroom graphics, research datasets and automated data products: official numbers can be joined correctly at the database level and still answer different statistical questions.

The correction is not to avoid comparison. It is to make the comparison object explicit and auditable.

Interpretation boundary

This note does not claim that Saudi Arabia and Jordan use identical labour-force survey methods once nationality scope is aligned. The scope-aligned pairings above are deliberately coded CAUTION, not PASS, until the remaining comparability fields are checked. It also does not rank labour-market performance or infer causes of unemployment-rate differences.
