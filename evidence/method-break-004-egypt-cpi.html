Method Break 004 · Egypt · Prices

A CPI base change is not a license to splice index levels.

Egypt introduced its tenth Consumer Price Index series from October 2019 using 2018/19 as the base period. Long historical work therefore needs explicit base-regime handling.

What changed

CAPMAS introduced the tenth CPI series from October 2019 and adopted 2018/19 as the new base period, replacing the preceding CPI base regime.

Why this matters

Index levels are normalized objects. A value of 150 under one base regime is not directly comparable to 150 under another simply because the label is still “CPI.” For long historical analysis, the base and series version have to be part of the observation.

User rule

Do not splice raw CPI index levels across base regimes. Use one official linked/rebased series where available, or explicitly rebase before comparing levels. For inflation-rate analysis, use producer-supported rates and preserve the series version used.

Source trail

Primary producer: Central Agency for Public Mobilization and Statistics (CAPMAS).

  • CAPMAS metadata for the tenth CPI series

Claim boundary

This note does not claim a structural break in Egyptian inflation itself. It documents a statistical base/series change and the handling required to keep index-level comparisons defensible.
